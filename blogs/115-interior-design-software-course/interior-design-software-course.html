One practical learning sequence is: AutoCAD first for accurate 2D layouts and working drawings, then SketchUp for 3D design, then one real-time renderer such as Enscape, D5 or Twinmotion, then Photoshop for boards, and finally Revit for coordinated BIM drawing sets. Most of these are free or cheap for students. A good course teaches each tool through a real interior project, not just commands. Below is why this order works, what each course should cover, a 16-week plan, 2026 costs, hardware needs and how to pick a course.

TL;DR

  • Start with AutoCAD: layouts, ceiling plans, elevations and details are what junior designers produce every day.
  • Add SketchUp early for quick 3D design and client presentations.
  • Learn one renderer well, not four badly.
  • Photoshop and a layout tool turn your work into boards and a portfolio.
  • Revit comes last but matters most for larger projects and Gulf jobs, where coordinated BIM drawings are expected.
  • Choose courses by projects and feedback, not by the length of the software list.

Why This Order Works

  1. AutoCAD first because accurate 2D drawing teaches scale, dimensions, line weights and drawing conventions, which every other tool assumes you know. It is also what most offices test first.
  2. SketchUp second because once you can draw a plan accurately, turning it into 3D is quick, and 3D sharpens your design decisions.
  3. A renderer third because rendering only looks good when the model underneath is clean and the materials are real.
  4. Photoshop fourth because presentation pulls everything together for clients and your portfolio.
  5. Revit last because it combines drawing, modelling and data; it is much easier once you understand drawings and 3D separately.

If you already work in an office that uses Revit as its main tool, move Revit earlier: learn what your team uses first.

A 16-Week Learning Plan With Practice Projects

  1. Weeks 1–4: AutoCADDraw a 2BHK you know from measurements: furniture layout, flooring plan, RCP with lights, electrical layout, two room elevations and a wardrobe detail, plotted to PDF.
  2. Weeks 3–7: SketchUpModel the same flat in 3D from your CAD plan: furniture, joinery, ceilings and materials; save scenes for each room; make one LayOut sheet.
  3. Weeks 6–9: RendererRender three stills (living by day, bedroom by night, kitchen) and a 30-second walkthrough of your SketchUp model.
  4. Weeks 8–10: Photoshop and layoutPost-produce the renders, make a material board and lay out a four-page project spread for your portfolio.
  5. Weeks 10–16: RevitRebuild the flat in Revit with rooms, finishes, furniture families and ceilings; produce interior elevations and a room finish schedule on titled sheets.

Using one project throughout is deliberate: you learn each tool without redesigning, and you finish with a complete, portfolio-ready project shown in four tools.

Why Revit Belongs in Your Plan

Many interior designers stop at SketchUp and AutoCAD, and for small residential work that can be enough. But on offices, hotels and retail fit-outs, and especially on Gulf projects, firms increasingly work in BIM. In Revit, rooms carry finishes and areas, furniture is counted in schedules, ceilings and lights sit in the ceiling plan, and elevations stay consistent with the plan when things move. Since 1 January 2024, Dubai has required BIM model submissions in IFC format for building permits on larger buildings, as summarised on Autodesk's Dubai BIM mandate page, which is why Revit appears in so many Gulf interior job adverts.

Revit is also the tool people find hardest to learn alone, because the challenge is the workflow (templates, views, schedules, coordination) rather than the buttons. A structured option such as Augmintech's PG Program in BIM Architecture is one way to reach that stage with guidance.

A Weekly Practice Routine That Works

  • Five short sessions beat one long one: an hour a day keeps commands and shortcuts fresh.
  • One real room per week: measure a room at home or in a friend's flat and draw it properly.
  • Copy a professional drawing: redraw a published elevation or detail to learn line weights and annotation.
  • Time yourself: practise a one-hour furniture layout, as many interviews include one.
  • Get feedback: share work with a mentor, classmate or instructor every week.

Common Mistakes When Learning Interior Software

  1. Learning five tools at once, mastering none.
  2. Starting with rendering before you can model and draw accurately.
  3. Downloading heavy models from online libraries and wondering why files crawl.
  4. Skipping working drawings, the skill employers test first.
  5. Using student licences for paid work, which their terms do not allow.
  6. Never finishing a project, so there is nothing to show at interviews.

Use One Interior Project Across the Tools

Choose a small living-dining room and keep its dimensions, openings and furniture schedule consistent. In AutoCAD, create the checked plan and plot it. In SketchUp, build organised geometry and save review scenes. In one renderer, develop lighting and materials. In Photoshop, assemble a presentation while retaining the source visual and adjustment layers. If your target role is BIM-focused, develop the documentation in Revit earlier in the sequence.

The order is a learning option, not a universal prerequisite. Someone joining a Revit-based interior team can learn Revit directly while practising drawing conventions. A visualisation specialist may need deeper modelling and lighting skills before broader BIM coordination.

Three Handover Problems to Practise

  • Wrong scale: compare a known dimension after import and confirm source units before modelling.
  • Missing materials: keep texture files in a consistent project folder and learn the renderer’s resource collection or relinking workflow.
  • Revision mismatch: record which model produced each visual and PDF. Re-export affected views after the design changes.

Measure Progress by Outputs

A beginner should be able to produce a readable layout, an organised model and a simple presentation without following every click in a tutorial. The next milestone is a complete room package: ceiling plan, internal elevations, finish schedule and one developed joinery detail. Then test one design revision and explain what updated automatically and what needed manual checking.

For a course comparison, ask how often your files are reviewed, what the final project includes and whether feedback leads to resubmission. Check licence eligibility and host-plugin compatibility before buying equipment or software. A list of applications is less informative than a project you can explain from brief to issued output.

Showing Software Skills to Employers

  • List only what your pages prove: if you write Revit, include a Revit sheet or schedule.
  • Show one project across tools: AutoCAD drawings, SketchUp model, render and Revit sheet of the same space.
  • Caption the tool under each image or drawing.
  • Be ready for a test: many interviews include a timed AutoCAD layout or SketchUp task.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which software should an interior designer learn first?
AutoCAD, for accurate 2D layouts and working drawings, followed by SketchUp for 3D design. Add a renderer, Photoshop and then Revit.
Is SketchUp enough for interior design?
For design and presentation on small projects, often yes, especially with LayOut. For full working drawing sets and larger or BIM projects, AutoCAD and Revit are usually needed too.
How long does it take to learn interior design software?
With one to two hours a day, our suggested plan covers AutoCAD, SketchUp, a renderer, Photoshop and the basics of Revit in about 16 weeks. Real fluency comes from using them on projects afterwards.
Which renderer is best for interiors?
Enscape, D5, Twinmotion and V-Ray all produce strong interior renders. Twinmotion and D5 have free education licences and Enscape and V-Ray are US$149 a year in Chaos's student bundle. Learn one well.
Do interior designers need Revit?
Not for every job, but increasingly for commercial, hospitality and Gulf projects, where coordinated BIM drawing sets and schedules are expected.
Can I learn interior design software for free?
Largely, as a student: Autodesk tools are free through the education plan, Twinmotion and D5 have free education licences, and Autodesk offers free prep courses. Structured courses add projects and feedback.
Can I use a MacBook for interior design software?
Yes for SketchUp, AutoCAD for Mac and Enscape with SketchUp. Revit and D5 Render need Windows.
