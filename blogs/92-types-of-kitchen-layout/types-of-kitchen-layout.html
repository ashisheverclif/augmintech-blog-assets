The six types of kitchen layout are straight, L-shaped, U-shaped, parallel, island and peninsula. Each one below comes with a plan drawn to scale, the minimum room width it needs, and its pros and cons. If you are planning a modular kitchen for an actual flat, start with the comparison table, then check your choice against the work triangle and standard dimensions further down.

TL;DR

  • Straight and L-shaped layouts suit small kitchens, down to the 1.8 m minimum width India's building code sets for a kitchen.
  • U-shaped and parallel layouts need about 2.3 m of width to keep NKBA's 1,067 mm work aisle between counters.
  • Island and peninsula layouts need open-plan space, with clearance for seating as well as cooking.
  • The NKBA work triangle limits the sink-hob-fridge loop to 7.9 m, with each leg between 1.2 and 2.7 m.
  • Indian counters are usually about 860 mm high and 600 mm deep, with wall cabinets 450 to 600 mm above.
  • Gulf flats often have open kitchens, but heavy Indian-style cooking works better in a closed kitchen with ducted exhaust.

Straight kitchen

Sample 3.0 × 1.8 m

Everything on one wall: fridge, sink, then hob. A modular kitchen here is a single run of base and wall units.

Pro: cheapest and fits the smallest rooms. Con: the triangle flattens into a line, and counter space runs out fast.

L-shaped kitchen

Sample 3.0 × 2.7 m

Counters on two adjoining walls. The most common layout in Indian flats, and easy to open to a dining area.

Pro: a natural triangle across the corner. Con: the corner unit needs a carousel or blind-corner fitting to be usable.

U-shaped kitchen

Sample 3.0 × 2.7 m

Counters on three walls, with the sink often under the window and hob and fridge on the side runs.

Pro: the most counter and storage of any layout. Con: two corner units, and it feels cramped if the gap between legs is under 1.07 m.

Parallel kitchen

Sample 3.0 × 2.4 m

Two facing runs with an aisle between, also called a galley kitchen. Often sink on one side, hob on the other.

Pro: very efficient, with short steps between runs. Con: with doors at both ends it becomes a corridor.

Island kitchen

Sample 4.2 × 3.9 m

A wall run plus a free-standing island, which can hold the sink or hob and a breakfast counter.

Pro: social, with extra prep space and seating. Con: needs a large room, and a sink or hob on the island needs plumbing or exhaust routed through the floor or ceiling.

Peninsula kitchen

Sample 3.9 × 3.0 m

An L or U with one counter jutting into the room, open on its end, with seating on the far side. Sometimes called a G-shape.

Pro: island-style seating in less space. Con: it can block the route into the kitchen.

The Kitchen Work Triangle

The work triangle connects the three places you move between most: the sink, the hob and the fridge. If the legs are too short, you are cramped; too long, and you walk too much. The National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA) sets these limits in its Kitchen Planning Guidelines:

7.9 m26 ft · Guideline 3Maximum total of the three legs.
1.2–2.7 m4–9 ft · Guideline 3Minimum and maximum for each leg.
305 mm12 in · Guideline 3No leg should cut through an island or peninsula by more than this.
No trafficGuideline 5Major walking routes should not cross the triangle.

Every sample plan above passes: their legs run from 1.2 to 2.6 m and their totals from 5.4 to 6.3 m. NKBA also asks for counter space beside each work point, called landing areas: at least 610 mm on one side of the sink and 457 mm on the other (Guideline 11); 305 mm on one side of the hob and 381 mm on the other (Guideline 17); and 381 mm on the handle side of the fridge (Guideline 16). A dishwasher should sit within 914 mm of the sink (Guideline 13).

Drawing a Kitchen Layout Plan

A kitchen drawing set needs at least a dimensioned plan and an elevation of each wall. Here is the L-shaped sample as a plan, and its sink wall as an elevation.

Measure and draw the shell. Walls, doors, windows, columns and beams from site measurements, plus water, drain, gas and electrical points.
Set up layers. Keep walls, casework, appliances and dimensions apart, for example A-WALL, I-CASE, I-APPL and A-ANNO-DIMS.
Offset the counter. Draw the counter line 600 mm off the wall and split it into modules, starting with the corner unit.
Place sink, hob and fridge. Insert blocks, then check each triangle leg and the aisle against the NKBA figures.
Project the elevations. Draw each wall with plinth, base units, the 860 mm counter, the gap, wall units and the chimney.
Annotate. Add module widths, appliance sizes, finishes, socket and switch positions, and a title block with scale.

Kitchen Layouts in Gulf Apartments

Many newer UAE apartments come with open-plan kitchens. Bayut's guide names Jumeirah Village Circle and Jumeirah Lake Towers as areas known for them, often with an island or breakfast counter facing the living room. The same guide warns that in an open kitchen, cooking smells spread through the home, and that frequent cooks often prefer a closed kitchen so the rest of the house is unaffected.

Open kitchen with island

  • Suits light cooking and entertaining
  • Plan NKBA seating clearance behind island stools
  • Put the hob on the wall run, under a ducted hood, rather than on the island

Closed kitchen

  • Suits daily Indian-style cooking: tempering, frying and spices
  • Fit a ducted chimney 650–750 mm above the gas hob
  • A glass partition or sliding door keeps the open feel without the smells

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the types of kitchen layout?
The six types are straight (single wall), L-shaped, U-shaped, parallel (galley), island and peninsula. They differ in how many walls the counters use and whether a free-standing or projecting counter is added.
What is the kitchen work triangle?
It is the imaginary triangle joining the sink, hob and fridge. NKBA guidelines say the three legs should total no more than 7.9 m (26 ft), each leg should be 1.2 to 2.7 m (4 to 9 ft), and major traffic should not pass through it.
Which kitchen layout is best for small flats?
A straight or L-shaped layout. Both fit kitchens as narrow as India's 1.8 m minimum width, and the L-shape still gives a working triangle across the corner.
What is the standard kitchen counter height in India?
About 860 mm (34 in) is most common, with 860 to 915 mm considered ideal (HouseYog, May 2026). Taller cooks may want the higher end, and the counter should be set after choosing the hob, since a built-on hob adds height.
What is the difference between a parallel and an L-shaped kitchen?
A parallel kitchen has two facing counters with an aisle between them, so it needs about 2.3 m of width for a 1,067 mm aisle. An L-shaped kitchen uses two adjoining walls, so it fits narrower rooms and leaves one side open to dining or a door.
