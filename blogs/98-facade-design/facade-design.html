Facade design is the design of a building's outer face, balancing how it looks with how it handles light, heat, weather and cost. This guide is about building facades, not the software "facade design pattern". It covers the six main types of facade, how their materials compare, what they cost in India and the Gulf, and how to design and model them.

TL;DR

  • A facade keeps out weather, controls heat and light, frames views and gives the building its identity.
  • The six common types are glass, ACP, stone, brick, green and double-skin facades.
  • ACP is the cheapest modern cladding in India, at about ₹160 to ₹450 per sq ft installed in 2026.
  • Structural glazing costs the most, at about ₹800 to ₹2,500+ per sq ft installed.
  • In hot climates, shading and orientation matter more than material: fins, screens and less west-facing glass cut heat gain.
  • ACP with a plastic (polyethylene) core can spread fire; UAE codes tightened after a series of tower fires.
  • In Revit, facades are modelled with curtain systems and panel families, so every panel can be counted and scheduled.

What Is Facade Design?

A facade is the exterior face of a building, usually the front, though designers use the word for every outside wall. Facade design decides what that face is made of, how it is put together and how it performs. A good facade does five jobs at once:

WeatherKeeps out rain, wind and dust
HeatLimits solar gain and heat loss
LightLets daylight in without glare
ViewsFrames the outlook, keeps privacy
IdentityGives the building its character

Facade, cladding and envelope are related but not the same. The envelope is everything that separates inside from outside, including the roof and ground floor. The facade is the visible outer wall within that envelope. Cladding is the outer layer fixed onto a facade, such as ACP, stone or terracotta panels hung on a sub-frame. A glass curtain wall, by contrast, is both the facade and the wall itself. Knowing the difference matters when you read a specification: a cladding package is usually priced and installed separately from the wall behind it.

6 Types of Facades

1. Glass facade

Glass in an aluminium frame (curtain wall) or bonded flush with structural silicone. It gives daylight and views and the classic corporate look, but without solar control glass and shading it lets in a lot of heat.

OfficesTowersShowroomsHeat gain

2. ACP facade

Aluminium composite panels: two thin aluminium skins bonded to a core, routed, folded and fixed to a sub-frame. Light, flat, colourful and quick to fit. The core matters: plastic cores burn, so fire-rated cores are now specified.

RetailCommercialSignage bandsCheck core

3. Stone facade

Natural stone slabs, such as granite, sandstone or limestone, fixed with mechanical anchors or as a ventilated rainscreen. Durable and non-combustible, with a premium look, but heavy and dependent on good fixings.

VillasHotelsInstitutionsHeavy

4. Brick and terracotta facade

Exposed load-bearing brick, brick tiles or terracotta rainscreen panels. Brick can be laid as a jaali, a perforated screen that shades and ventilates. Low-tech, local and long-lasting.

HomesCampusesLow-riseLabour skill

5. Green facade

A green facade is climbers grown on cables or mesh from the ground; a living wall has plants in modular panels on the wall. NBS notes established greenery can filter out around 80% of solar radiation. Living walls need irrigation and regular maintenance.

OfficesMallsHomesMaintenance

6. Double-skin facade

Two layers of facade with an air cavity between them, often holding blinds. Air moving through the cavity carries heat away. A 2023 simulation for hot-climate Erbil found suitable double-skin designs cut annual cooling demand by 9 to 14%.

Studio Matrx's guide names the usual cost drivers for each system. For ACP, a fire-rated core, a PVDF coating, branded panels and the sub-frame push the rate up. For glazing, unitised systems, laminated or double-glazed units and spider fittings do. For stone, imported granite or limestone, thick slabs and mechanical anchors do. Building height adds access costs on top of every system.

Treat all of these as planning bands. Facade prices move with aluminium and glass prices, panel size, fixing system, building height and access, so always get itemised quotes.

Designing a Facade for Climate

In India and the Gulf the facade's biggest job is keeping out the sun without losing daylight. More glass means more daylight and views, but also more heat and glare, and the cooling plant pays for every extra square metre. The aim is to let in diffuse light while stopping direct sun before it reaches the glass. Four decisions do most of the work:

OrientationPut most glass on the north and south faces; keep east and west glass small, because low morning and evening sun is hard to block.
Shading devicesHorizontal overhangs and louvres block high sun; vertical fins block low side sun. Fit them outside the glass.
Glazing ratioEvery square metre of glass is a heat load. Size windows for daylight and views, and use solar control glass where large panes are needed.
Screens: jaali and mashrabiyaPerforated stone, brick or timber screens let air and dappled light through while cutting glare and giving privacy.

Modelling Facades in Revit

Revit has three main ways to model a facade, from simple to complex. (Flat glass walls are covered in our curtain wall guide.)

Curtain systemsApply a grid of panels and mullions to any face, including sloped or curved mass faces, using the Curtain System by Face tool.For: glazed roofs, curved glass, ACP grids
Curtain panel pattern familiesDivide a mass surface with a U/V grid and fill it with a Curtain Panel Pattern Based family, with sizes and materials as parameters.For: patterned and parametric facades
Adaptive componentsFamilies whose points stick to a grid and flex with it, so each panel can fold or open differently.For: twisted panels, varying fins and screens

Whichever you use, schedule the panels: a curtain panel schedule counts panel types, areas and materials, and shared parameters can carry panel IDs, fire ratings and coatings, which is exactly what the facade contractor will ask for.

Facades on Gulf Projects

Facade fire safety reshaped Gulf cladding rules. A string of high-rise fires showed how flames could race up the outside of a building through combustible cladding:

  • 2012Fire at Al Baker Tower, Sharjah, the start of a series of more than 11 high-rise fires in the UAE, according to Gulf News.
  • 2013The UAE Fire and Life Safety Code required fire-resistant cladding on new buildings over 15 m (Cavendish Maxwell).
  • 2015Fires at the Address Downtown hotel on New Year's Eve and at the Torch tower in Dubai.
  • 2017An updated code required non-combustible cladding on new buildings across the UAE, fire-retardant mineral cores, and testing of the whole facade system, not just the panel (The National).
  • 2018Manufacturers began halting polyethylene-core panels, as revisions requiring A2 fire-rated panels were proposed (Gulf News).

Under the code as described by Vortex Fire, each facade component must be reaction-to-fire tested and the complete assembly tested at full scale, a Civil Defence-approved fire consultant reviews the design, and the cladding supplier, system and installer must be registered with Civil Defence. Field inspections run at least every 20% of installation.

What this means for ACP: panels with a plastic (polyethylene) core are the main problem, and the 2017 code calls for non-combustible cladding with fire-retardant mineral cores on new buildings, tested as a whole system. Manufacturers now sell fire-retardant and A2-grade ACP, but the classification your building needs depends on its type and height, so confirm it with the current code, Civil Defence and a fire consultant before specifying.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the types of facade?
The six common types are glass facades (curtain wall or structural glazing), ACP facades, stone facades, brick and terracotta facades, green facades and living walls, and double-skin facades.
What is facade design in architecture?
Facade design is the design of a building's outer face: its materials, layout and details, chosen to keep out weather, control heat and light, frame views and give the building its character, within the budget.
What is a double-skin facade?
A double-skin facade has two layers, usually glass, separated by an air cavity that may hold blinds. Air moving through the cavity carries heat away; one 2023 hot-climate study found suitable designs cut cooling demand by 9 to 14%.
Which facade material is best for hot climates?
Heavy, non-combustible materials such as stone, brick and terracotta, used with shading devices and screens such as jaali or mashrabiya. Where glass is needed, use solar control glass and keep it off the east and west faces.
Is ACP cladding fire safe?
It depends on the core. ACP with a polyethylene (plastic) core can ignite and spread fire quickly. Fire-retardant and A2 mineral-core grades perform far better, and in the UAE the whole facade system must be tested and approved through Civil Defence.
