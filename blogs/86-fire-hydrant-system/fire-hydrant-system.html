Short answer

A building fire hydrant system is a network of pumps, pipes and valves that supplies pressurised water to hose points, so firefighters and trained occupants can fight a fire inside and around the building.

This page is about hydrant systems inside building premises, not the red street hydrants on public roads. The labelled diagram below shows the whole system at a glance, from the water tank to the hose.

TL;DR

  • A hydrant system stores water, pumps it, and delivers it under pressure to hose outlets inside and outside a building.
  • Water flows from the tank to the fire pumps, through a ring main, to wet risers and yard hydrants, then out through landing valves and hoses.
  • The five main components are the fire pump, ring main, landing valve, hose box and fire brigade inlet.
  • Yard hydrants protect the outside of a building; internal hydrants (wet risers) serve each floor.
  • Indian codes call for at least 3.5 kg/cm² at the hydraulically most remote hydrant (IS 13039 and NBC 2016 Part 4).
  • Saudi SBC 801 and UAE practice follow NFPA; NFPA 14 asks for 100 psi residual at the most remote standpipe hose valves.

What Is a Fire Hydrant System?

A fire hydrant system in a building is a permanently installed, water-filled fire fighting system: a dedicated water store, fire pumps, a pipe network, and hose outlets placed so that every part of the building can be reached by a hose. It is part of the "F" in MEPF.

Building hydrant system (this page)Private system within a site: tank, pumps, ring main, wet risers, landing valves, hose boxes and yard hydrants, designed to NBC, IS, NFPA or local fire codes.
Street hydrant (not this page)Public hydrant on a municipal water main, used by the fire brigade to fill tenders. This is what most encyclopedia pages describe.

How a Fire Hydrant System Works

A hydrant system is kept full of water and pressurised all the time, so water is available the moment a valve is opened. The water path:

  1. 01
    Stored water

    Dedicated fire-water reserve supplies the pumps.

  2. 02
    Pressure and pumping

    Jockey pump maintains pressure; fire pumps supply the fire demand.

  3. 03
    Distribution loop

    The ring main carries water around the building.

  4. 04
    Indoor and outdoor outlets

    Wet risers supply floor outlets; branches feed yard hydrants.

  5. 05
    Hose connection

    Firefighters connect a hose at the landing valve.

When a landing valve is opened, line pressure falls and the pressure switches start the main electric pump. If it fails, a diesel standby pump starts. The fire brigade can also pump water into the system through the fire brigade inlet.

5 Main Components

  1. Fire pump set

    Usually a main electric pump, a diesel standby pump of the same capacity and a small jockey pump to keep the system pressurised. NBC 2016 Table 7 notes refer to sets with electric and diesel pumps, including 2 850 litre/min diesel standby and 180 litre/min jockey pumps for taller buildings.

    Ref: NBC 2016 Part 4, Table 7 (notes)

  2. Ring main

    The main pipe is laid as a ring (loop), so water can reach any hydrant from two directions and a section can be isolated for repair without shutting down the whole system. IS 13039 says hydrant mains "should always be laid in rings or crosslinked rings".

    Ref: IS 13039:1991, clause 5.4

  3. Landing valve

    The hose outlet valve on each floor of a wet riser and at each yard hydrant. Landing valves come in single and double outlet types, typically 63 mm, to IS 5290.

    Ref: IS 5290:1993, Specification for landing valves

  4. Hose box and hose reel

    A cabinet beside the landing valve holding fire hoses and a branch pipe (nozzle), so firefighters do not need to carry hose up the building. A first-aid hose reel is usually provided next to it for occupants.

    Ref: IS 3844:1989, internal hydrants and hose reels

  5. Fire brigade inlet

    An inlet connection at ground level, usually with several instantaneous couplings, through which fire brigade tenders can pump water into the ring main or riser if the building's own supply runs short.

The Indian standards that cover hydrant systems are:

  • NBC 2016 Part 4 (Fire and Life Safety): Table 7 sets which buildings need hydrants, wet risers, tanks and pumps, by occupancy and height.
  • IS 13039:1991: External hydrant systems, provision and maintenance (code of practice).
  • IS 3844:1989: Installation and maintenance of internal hydrants and hose reels on premises.
  • IS 5290:1993: Specification for landing valves.
Code update: NBCS 2026. BIS has published the National Building Construction Standards 2026 (SP 7:2026), reported as effective from 30 April 2026 and replacing NBC 2016 (ULBA; Drishti IAS). A comparison by Sparrow RMS reports that the 3.5 bar minimum at the remotest hydrant is unchanged, with a new 7.0 bar maximum and pump capacities now calculated from protection levels (Sparrow RMS). Hydrant Requirements in the UAE and Saudi Arabia

India: NBC 2016 Part 4

  • Table 7 sets hydrants, risers, tanks and pumps by occupancy and height
  • 3.5 kg/cm² at the most remote hydrant
  • IS 13039, IS 3844 and IS 5290 for design and equipment
  • NBCS 2026 now published

UAE: Fire and Life Safety Code

  • UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice, 2018 edition, enforced by Civil Defence
  • Hydrant systems designed to the code and NFPA 24; pumps to NFPA 20
  • Abu Dhabi Civil Defence issued 54 local amendments on 12 March 2026, covering fire pumps and hydrants

Saudi Arabia: SBC 801

  • Saudi Fire Code, based on the 2015 International Fire Code with NFPA standards; revised 2018
  • Section 507 and Appendix C: water supplies and hydrant locations
  • Section 905 standpipes, 912 fire department connections, 913 fire pumps
  • Standpipes to NFPA 14

UAE. The UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice (2018 edition) governs hydrant systems, which are designed with hydraulic calculations to the code and NFPA 24, with pumps to NFPA 20 (MMJ). In Abu Dhabi, the Civil Defence Authority released 54 amendments on 12 March 2026 affecting fire pumps and hydrant requirements. One example: for industrial buildings, private hydrant systems may be waived where public hydrants are available within 100 m (Vortex Fire).

Saudi Arabia. SBC 801 places fire water supplies and hydrant locations in Section 507 and Appendix C, and standpipes, fire department connections and fire pumps in Sections 905, 912 and 913 (SBC 801 contents, 2018). A 2025 comparative study notes that standpipes follow NFPA 14, with Class III for buildings over 9 m, and that on-site hydrants may be required where a facility is more than 120 m from a hydrant, or 180 m if sprinklered (Pillai, ASRJETS 2025).

NFPA 14 numbers. Under NFPA 14, a Class I standpipe system needs a minimum residual pressure of 100 psi (about 6.9 bar) at the two most remote 2½ in hose valves, with 500 gpm for the first standpipe and 250 gpm for each additional one (NFSA; San Francisco Fire Department, citing NFPA 14-2019). That is roughly twice the Indian minimum of 3.5 kg/cm² (about 3.4 bar), so always design to the code the project is approved under.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a fire hydrant system work?
Water is stored in a dedicated tank and kept under pressure by a jockey pump. When a landing valve or hydrant is opened, pressure drops and the main fire pump starts, pushing water through the ring main and risers to the hose. A diesel pump takes over if the electric pump fails, and the fire brigade can pump in through the fire brigade inlet.
What are the main components of a fire hydrant system?
The fire pump set (main, diesel standby and jockey pumps), the ring main, landing valves, hose boxes with hose reels, and the fire brigade inlet, plus the water tank and wet risers.
What is the difference between a yard hydrant and an internal hydrant?
A yard hydrant is outside the building on the ring main and protects the building from outside. An internal hydrant is a landing valve on a wet riser inside the building, one per floor, used to fight fires floor by floor.
What pressure is required in a fire hydrant system?
In India, at least 3.5 kg/cm² at the hydraulically most remote hydrant (IS 13039 and NBC 2016 Part 4), and 5.25 kg/cm² for high-hazard occupancies under IS 13039. Under NFPA 14, used in the Gulf, a Class I standpipe needs 100 psi residual at the most remote hose valves.
What is a landing valve?
A landing valve is the hose outlet valve on a wet riser or hydrant, usually 63 mm, single or double outlet, to which firefighters connect a hose. In India it is specified by IS 5290.
