AutoCADRevit

AutoCAD is drafting software: you draw each plan, elevation and section as separate lines. Revit is BIM software: you build one model of walls, floors and doors, and model-based views are generated from it, supplemented by drafting details and annotations. That one difference decides how fast you can produce drawings, how safely you can make changes, and what kind of projects each tool suits. Here is the comparison, side by side.

CAD · draftingAutoCADLines, arcs, blocks and layers in 2D (and 3D). Fast, flexible and universal. Every view is drawn and updated by hand.
BIM · modellingRevitWalls, doors, floors and rooms as intelligent objects in one model. Plans, sections, elevations and schedules all come from that model.

TL;DR

  • AutoCAD draws lines; Revit models building objects that carry height, materials and other data.
  • In Revit, a change made once updates every plan, section, elevation and schedule; in AutoCAD you edit each drawing.
  • AutoCAD is quicker to learn and better for small projects and details; Revit has a steeper learning curve.
  • Revit wins on larger projects where many people work on one model and schedules must stay accurate.
  • Revit costs more: US$3,005 a year against US$2,095 for AutoCAD at 2026 US list prices.
  • Most offices use both: Revit for the model and set, AutoCAD for details and DWG exchange.

Modeling: Lines vs Objects

In AutoCAD, a wall on a plan is usually two parallel lines on a wall layer. AutoCAD knows they are lines, not that they are a wall, so it has no idea how tall the wall is, what it is made of, or which window sits in it. In Revit, the same wall is one object with a type, a height, its material layers and parameters such as a fire rating, and it hosts the window placed in it.

AutoCAD can work in 3D, and its Architecture toolset adds wall and door objects, but most offices use AutoCAD for 2D drafting. Revit is designed around the 3D model from the start: its core is a parametric change engine, so changes propagate through model relationships; joined or constrained elements still need review.

Both programs produce professional drawing sets with title blocks and sheets. The difference is where the information comes from. In AutoCAD, a door schedule is a table someone typed or built from block attributes, and it is only as accurate as the last person who updated it. In Revit, each view, schedule and dimension is derived from the model's database, so a door schedule reads model elements, subject to its category, phase, filters and populated parameters.

Project Changes: The One-Wall Test

Changes are where the two programs differ most. Take a simple client request: move an internal wall 300 mm to make a bedroom bigger.

AutoCAD Several coordinated edits

  • Move the wall lines on the floor plan
  • Fix the furniture and door positions
  • Update the dimensions
  • Redraw the wall on the section
  • Update the room areas table
  • Check the ceiling and electrical plans by hand

Revit Model edit + review

  • Move the wall once in any view
  • Plan, section and 3D update
  • Dimensions update
  • Room areas and schedules update
  • Doors hosted in the wall move with it
  • Review ceiling boundaries, hosted fittings and dimensions

Illustrative workflow, not a timed test. In practice you still check every sheet in Revit, but you are checking, not redrawing.

On a single house, the AutoCAD edits take a few minutes. On a hotel with hundreds of rooms and dozens of sheets, the same kind of change, made dozens of times through a project, is where Revit saves the most time and errors.

The pattern is clear. AutoCAD is quick for the first plan, because drawing lines is fast. Revit is slower for the first plan, because you set wall types, levels and families, but every drawing after that costs much less. The more drawings and revisions a project has, the more Revit pays back that set-up time.

Working Together: DWG, Revit and IFC

You rarely have to choose one for life. The two programs work together:

DWG into RevitRevit links or imports AutoCAD drawings, such as a survey or site plan. Autodesk recommends linking in most cases, so the file can be reloaded when it changes.
Revit to DWGRevit views and sheets export to DWG for consultants and contractors who work in AutoCAD.
WorksharingIn Revit, several team members can work on one central model at the same time, over a network or the cloud.
IFCRevit exports IFC, the open BIM format some authorities, such as Dubai Municipality, require for permit submissions.

Watch the version numbers

One practical trap catches many teams. Autodesk's help states that Revit models are not backwards-compatible: once you open and save a model in a newer release, you can no longer open it in an earlier one. If your consultant is on Revit 2025 and you are on 2027, they cannot open your file. Agree on one Revit release for the whole project before modelling starts, and keep backups before any upgrade. AutoCAD is easier here, because a drawing can be saved in an older DWG format for someone on an older version.

The Middle Ground: AutoCAD's Architecture Toolset

Many people do not realise that full AutoCAD (not AutoCAD LT) includes the Architecture toolset. Autodesk describes it as adding more than 8,800 architectural components, placing walls, doors and windows as real construction objects, and automatically generating floor plans, elevations, sections and ceiling grids. Autodesk claims it can raise drafting productivity by up to 61%; that figure comes from studies Autodesk commissioned, comparing basic AutoCAD with the toolsets for experienced users, so treat it as the vendor's own estimate.

The toolset is a useful step for an AutoCAD office that is not ready for Revit. But it is still drawing-based, not a full BIM model: its project and collaboration workflow differs from Revit's. AutoCAD Architecture does support IFC import and export; exchange settings and recipient requirements still need checking. If your clients or authorities ask for BIM, the toolset does not replace Revit.

Revit or Revit LT?

The AutoCAD Revit LT Suite looks like a bargain at US$680 a year, and for a one- or two-person architecture studio it can be. But Autodesk's own comparison lists several things Revit LT does not include, and some of them matter a lot:

Missing in Revit LTWhy it matters
Worksharing (multi-user model)Only one person can work on a model at a time, so LT does not suit teams
In-product rendering, sun and shadow studiesLT has cloud rendering instead; most offices use Enscape or Twinmotion either way
View filters and in-place modellingEveryday tools for graphics control and one-off elements
Dynamo, add-ins and macrosNo automation or third-party plugins
Structure and MEP tools, point cloudsArchitecture only; no scan-to-BIM work

Rule of thumb: Revit LT suits a solo architect doing small buildings who mainly needs drawings from a model. If you work in a team, coordinate with consultants in Revit, or want plugins, you need full Revit.

Switching From AutoCAD to Revit: 7 Habits to Unlearn

Most AutoCAD users struggle with Revit not because it is hard, but because their drafting habits work against it. These are the most common ones.

  1. Drawing walls with lines. Detail lines look like walls but are not in the model, so they will not appear in sections, 3D or schedules. Use the Wall tool.
  2. Thinking in layers. Revit controls what you see by category and view settings (Visibility/Graphics), not by layers. Hide things per view, not by deleting them.
  3. Editing each view separately. Change the model, not the drawing. If you are fixing the same thing in two views, something is wrong.
  4. Exploding imported DWGs. Exploding a DWG inside Revit fills the model with thousands of loose lines. Link it and trace over it instead.
  5. Skipping the template. Wall types, text styles, title blocks and view settings belong in a project template. Set it up once and every project starts right.
  6. Modelling every detail in 3D. Model what changes and must be counted; draw small construction details as 2D detail components on top of sections.
  7. Working alone in a team file. On shared projects, use worksharing and synchronise often, instead of passing copies of the file around.

When to Use Which

Choose AutoCAD when

  • The project is small, such as a house or a single shop
  • You are drawing details, site plans or shop drawings
  • The client or contractor works in DWG
  • You are learning drafting for the first time

Choose Revit when

  • The project is large or repetitive: offices, hotels, hospitals
  • Many people and disciplines must work on one model
  • Schedules and quantities must stay accurate through changes
  • The client or authority asks for a BIM model or IFC

Microsol Resources sums it up the same way: AutoCAD remains common for single-family homes and limited-scope work, while Revit fits projects where a data-rich model adds value, and the choice also depends on the team's skills and the contract.

Revit and AutoCAD on Gulf Projects

The Gulf is moving faster towards BIM than many markets. Since 1 January 2024, Dubai has required BIM model submissions in IFC format for building permits on larger projects, including buildings over 20 floors or 20,000 m² for architectural designs, specialised buildings such as hospitals and universities, and government projects (as summarised on Autodesk's Dubai BIM mandate page). For architects and draftsmen planning a move to Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Riyadh, Revit skills are increasingly expected on large projects, while AutoCAD remains the common language for details, shop drawings and smaller fit-out work.

Practical tip: read the software list in each Gulf job advert. Many ask for both, so a portfolio that shows one project modelled in Revit and detailed in AutoCAD covers both bases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between Revit and AutoCAD?
AutoCAD is drafting software where you draw lines and blocks to make each drawing. Revit is BIM software where you build one model of building objects, and the plans, sections, elevations and schedules are generated from it and update together.
Is Revit better than AutoCAD?
For large, coordinated projects with many drawings and changes, yes. For small projects, quick layouts and detailed 2D drawings, AutoCAD is often faster. Most offices use both.
Should I learn AutoCAD before Revit?
It helps. AutoCAD teaches drafting conventions and drawing discipline that carry over to Revit, and you will still use DWG files when working with others. But you can learn Revit directly; it simply requires thinking in models rather than lines.
Can Revit open AutoCAD files?
Yes. Revit can link or import DWG files, and Autodesk recommends linking in most cases so the drawing can be reloaded when it changes. Revit can also export its views and sheets back to DWG.
Can I run Revit on a Mac?
Not directly. Revit runs only on Windows. Before considering a virtual machine, check Autodesk’s supported configuration for your exact Revit release, processor and virtualisation platform. AutoCAD has a native Mac version.
Can I open a newer Revit file in an older version?
No. Revit models are not backwards-compatible: once saved in a newer release, they cannot be opened in an earlier one. Agree on one Revit version for the whole project team.
Can Revit completely replace AutoCAD?
For producing a building's drawing set, yes; many offices model and document entirely in Revit. But AutoCAD remains useful for quick sketches, some 2D details and exchanging DWG files with consultants, so most offices keep both.
Is Revit LT enough?
For a solo architect on small projects, often yes. It lacks worksharing, view filters, in-place modelling, in-product rendering, Dynamo and add-ins, so teams and consultants usually need full Revit.
Is AutoCAD's Architecture toolset the same as Revit?
No. The toolset adds architectural objects and automatic plans, elevations and sections to AutoCAD, but it uses a different project and collaboration workflow. It also supports IFC import and export, so IFC alone is not the distinction.
Is Revit more expensive than AutoCAD?
Yes. At 2026 US list prices, Revit is US$3,005 a year and AutoCAD US$2,095 a year. AutoCAD LT is US$540 and the AutoCAD Revit LT Suite US$680. Students can use both free for learning through Autodesk's education plan.

A Practical Test Before You Choose

Use one small apartment brief in both tools: a dimensioned plan, one section, one door schedule and two issue sheets. Keep the same units, drawing scale and revision request. Record setup time separately from revision time; a first attempt is a learning exercise, not a productivity benchmark.

  1. Make one controlled revision: move a bedroom partition 300 mm, change one door width and update the room name.
  2. Check the drawing evidence: compare plan and section, door swing, dimension references, room boundaries and the schedule row. In Revit, model-driven information updates through valid relationships; manually written notes do not become correct automatically.
  3. Check the issued PDF: look for cropped dimensions, unreadable text, missing lineweights and the correct sheet revision. A correct model can still produce a poor sheet.
  4. Test handover: export a DWG, reopen it and check units, layers, fonts and coordinates. Send a trial file to your recipient before exporting the complete set.

Three Problems Beginners Commonly Meet

A linked DWG appears far away or at the wrong size: verify the source units and agreed origin before moving geometry. Use a known dimension to confirm scale. A Revit door is missing from a schedule: check the schedule category, phase, filters and whether the door belongs to a linked model. A view looks different on another sheet: compare its view template, scale, detail level and visibility settings before redrawing anything.
