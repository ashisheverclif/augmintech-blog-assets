A curtain wall is a non-load-bearing outer wall, usually glass in an aluminium frame, hung from a building's structure and carrying only its own weight and wind loads. This guide covers the four main curtain wall systems, the parts and how they fit together, the glass numbers that matter, and how to model one properly in Revit.

If you are an architecture student or a BIM modeller about to work on a glass-fronted building, this is the vocabulary and the modelling method you will need on day one.

TL;DR

  • A curtain wall hangs outside the floor slabs and carries only its own weight and wind, not floor loads.
  • The four main systems are stick, unitised, structural glazing and spider (point-fixed), in rising order of how frameless they look.
  • Stick systems are assembled on site; unitised panels arrive finished from the factory, which speeds up enclosure on tall buildings.
  • A mullion is the main vertical frame member; a transom is the horizontal one between panels.
  • For glass, read three numbers on the datasheet: U-value for heat loss, solar factor or SHGC for solar heat, and light transmission.
  • In Revit, model glazing as a curtain wall with grids, mullions and panels, never as a basic wall with a glass material.

What Is a Curtain Wall?

A curtain wall is the outer skin of a building, not part of its structure. Designing Buildings describes curtain walling as a non-loadbearing external wall system of glazed and opaque panels in a lightweight frame fixed to the building's primary structure. It is not entirely non-structural, though: the frame, fixings and glass must resist wind and their own weight, and brackets and anchors pass those loads back to the slabs or frame.

The floors, columns and core carry the building; the curtain wall only keeps out weather. That is why it can be almost entirely glass, and why it can run past many storeys without a break.

Curtain wall vs window wall

The two look similar from the street but sit differently on the structure. According to glazing contractor Kovach, a curtain wall attaches to the outside of the floor slabs and spans past them, while a window wall sits between the slabs, floor by floor. That leaves the window wall's slab edges exposed, usually covered with metal panels. Kovach adds that window wall works up to about 12 feet (3.7 m) floor to floor before its mullions need steel reinforcement, and that curtain wall suits taller spans and continuous glass towers.

4 Types of Curtain Wall Systems

SystemInstall methodSpeed on siteCost level (relative)Typical buildings
StickMullions and transoms fixed piece by piece on site, then glazedSlowest; needs access to both sidesLower direct costLow and mid-rise, podiums, shopfronts
UnitisedFactory-built, glazed panels lifted and hooked onto slab bracketsFastest enclosure, fewer site staffHigher direct costHigh-rise towers, repetitive façades
Structural glazingGlass bonded with structural silicone to carriers, fixed into the frameMedium; bonding done in the factoryHigherOffices wanting flush, frameless-looking glass

Stick vs unitised cost and speed: Building Design CPD on aluminium curtain walling ("higher direct costs" for unitised, which "hastens enclosure of the building" and needs fewer site staff). The other cost levels are relative and indicative only.

Stick system

The classic method. Profiles are cut and machined in the factory, then assembled on site as a kit: mullions slide onto base brackets at each floor, transoms fix between them, and the glass goes in last. Flexible when dimensions vary, but it needs scaffolding or access on both sides.

Unitised system

Whole panels, frame and glass together, are built and sealed in the factory, then lifted into place and hooked onto pre-positioned brackets on the slab. Better quality control and fewer site joints, at a higher direct cost.

Structural glazing

The glass is bonded to metal carriers with structural silicone sealant, usually in the factory, so no cap or pressure plate shows outside. The result is a smooth, all-glass face.

Spider (point-fixed) glazing

Large toughened panes are drilled and clamped at points by stainless steel "spider" fittings, often fixed to glass fins instead of mullions. Dimensions.com lists panes up to about 6.1 m tall for this system. Near-total transparency, with bespoke engineering.

Parts of a Curtain Wall

A mullion is the main vertical member of a curtain wall frame, running between floors and carrying the glass and wind loads back to the building through brackets. Its horizontal partner is the transom.
Anchor and bracketSteel or aluminium fixings cast into or bolted to the slab edge; they let the wall move with the building.
TransomThe horizontal frame member between mullions, supporting the glass above it.
Glazing panelUsually a double-glazed unit (DGU): two panes sealed around a gas-filled gap.
Spandrel panelAn opaque panel, often back-painted glass with insulation, that hides the slab and ceiling void.
GasketRubber (EPDM) strips that seal the glass to the frame and keep water out.
Pressure plate and capThe outside strip that clamps the glass to the frame, covered by a decorative cap.
Fire stopThe fire-rated seal between the slab edge and the wall, closing the gap between floors.
Vision glassThe clear glass zone between spandrels, at eye level.

Glass and Performance Basics

Most curtain walls use double glazing: two panes of glass sealed around a gap filled with air or argon. One pane carries a low-E coating, a microscopically thin metal layer that lets visible light through but reflects heat. When you read a glass datasheet, four numbers matter:

U-value (Ug)Heat flow through the glass, in W/m²K.Lower is better
Solar factor (g) / SHGCShare of the sun's heat that gets through.Lower is better in hot climates
Light transmissionShare of visible light that gets through.Higher means more daylight
SelectivityLight transmission divided by solar factor.Higher means more light per unit of heat

Solar factor (g) is the European measure under EN 410; solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) is the North American equivalent, measured slightly differently. Here are two real solar control double-glazed units:

How to Model a Curtain Wall in Revit

In Revit, a curtain wall is a wall type made of three parts that work together: curtain grids divide the wall, mullions sit on the grid lines, and curtain panels fill the spaces. Autodesk's help explains each.

  1. Pick a curtain wall type.Start the Architecture › Wall tool and choose a curtain wall type in the Type Selector. The default template includes Curtain Wall 1 (no grids), Exterior Glazing and Storefront.
  2. Set the type properties.Choose the default Curtain Panel, the vertical and horizontal grid layout and spacing, and the interior and border mullion types. Join Condition sets whether vertical or horizontal mullions run continuously.
  3. Draw the wall.Sketch it like any wall, with base and top constraints. Tick Automatically Embed in the type if you want a curtain wall to sit inside a basic wall like a large window.
  4. Add or edit curtain grids.Use Curtain Grid with All Segments, One Segment or All Except Picked, and remove pieces with Add/Remove Segments. Un-pin grids that come from the type before editing them.
  5. Add mullions.Use Mullion and place them on a Grid Line, a Grid Line Segment or All Grid Lines. The mullion's profile family controls its shape and size.
  6. Swap panels for spandrels, doors or custom panels.Hover over a panel, press Tab until it highlights, select it and pick a new type: a solid or empty panel, a basic wall for a spandrel, or a curtain wall door. A door fills the whole panel, so add grids first to size the panel to the door.
  7. Schedule it.Because panels and mullions are real elements, you can schedule them for quantities, sizes and glass types, which a façade contractor will ask for.

The common mistake

  • Drawing glass as a basic wall with a "Glass" material, or as a row of window families
  • No mullions, no panels, nothing to schedule
  • Doors cannot be hosted properly, and energy and IFC exports read it as a solid wall

Do this instead

  • Use a curtain wall type for vertical glazing
  • Use a Curtain System (by face) for sloped or curved glazing such as skylights
  • Keep spandrels, doors and vision glass as panel types you can swap and schedule

Curtain Walls on Gulf Projects

Many of the glass towers students will model are in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Riyadh. A 2025 study of Dubai façades (Salih, Emirati Journal of Civil Engineering and Applications) notes that the city's skyline is defined by the extensive use of glass in its towers, and that glass selection is key to thermal performance, solar gain and indoor comfort. In that sun, solar control glass is not optional.

Low solar factorChoose glass with a low g or SHGC. In the table above, 0.23 against 0.34 means about a third less solar heat through every square metre.
Codes set limitsDubai's Green Building Regulations and Specifications (2017) list a maximum SHGC among their mandatory requirements, per the World Bank's code database. Abu Dhabi uses Estidama and Saudi Arabia the Saudi Building Code energy requirements.
Check the current valuesLimits depend on building type and glazing ratio, and are revised. Take the actual U-value and SHGC limits from the current code text before you specify glass.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a curtain wall?
A curtain wall is a non-load-bearing outer wall, usually glass in an aluminium frame, hung from the building's structure. It carries only its own weight and wind loads, passing them to the floor slabs through brackets and anchors.
What is a mullion in a curtain wall?
A mullion is the main vertical member of the curtain wall frame. It spans between floors, holds the glass with the horizontal transoms, and transfers wind and self-weight to the structure through brackets.
What is the difference between stick and unitised curtain walls?
A stick curtain wall is assembled on site piece by piece: mullions, transoms, then glass. A unitised curtain wall arrives as factory-built, glazed panels that are lifted onto slab brackets. Unitised has higher direct costs but encloses the building faster with fewer site joints.
What is the difference between a curtain wall and a window wall?
A curtain wall hangs outside the floor slabs and runs continuously past them. A window wall sits between the slabs, floor by floor, leaving the slab edges to be covered separately.
How do you make a curtain wall in Revit?
Use the Wall tool with a curtain wall type, set its grid spacing and mullion types in Type Properties, draw it like a wall, then add curtain grids and mullions and swap panels for spandrels or doors as needed.
