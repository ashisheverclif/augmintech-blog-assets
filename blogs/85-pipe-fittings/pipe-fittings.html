TL;DR

  • Fittings change direction (elbows), branch (tees, crosses, olets), change size (reducers, swages) or close a line (caps).
  • Butt weld fittings follow ASME B16.9 (NPS 1/2 to 48) and match the pipe schedule.
  • Socket weld and threaded fittings follow ASME B16.11 (NPS 1/8 to 4) and are rated by class: 2000, 3000, 6000 or 9000.
  • The line class decides the fitting type, end connection and material for each size range.

What Are Pipe Fittings?

A pipe fitting is any component, other than straight pipe, flanges and valves, that joins pipe and does one of four jobs: change direction, branch the flow, change the pipe size, or close the end. On a piping isometric each fitting appears as a symbol, and on the MTO as a line item with its size, schedule or class, end type and material.

10 Types of Pipe Fittings with Names and Pictures

Elbows (45°, 90°, 180°; Long and Short Radius)

Elbows change direction. They come in 45°, 90° and 180° (return bend) versions, and in two radii (Project Materials):

  • Long radius (1.5D) is the default. A 90° LR elbow has a resistance coefficient of about 0.25 to 0.30, with less erosion, and ASME B31.3 assumes LR unless the designer specifies otherwise.
  • Short radius (1D) is used in tight spaces, where the spec calls for it, or in low-pressure utility service. Its K-factor is higher (about 0.35 to 0.45), and SR elbows can jam pipeline pigs.

Tees, Crosses and Olets

An equal tee has a branch the same size as the run; a reducing tee has a smaller branch. A cross adds a fourth opening, but is used far less in process piping. When the branch is much smaller than the run, an olet is welded straight onto the run pipe instead of cutting in a tee. Olets are integrally reinforced, so no separate reinforcing pad is needed, and are covered by MSS SP-97: weldolets for butt weld branches, sockolets for socket weld and threadolets for threaded instrument connections (Project Materials). The project's branch table in the line class says which to use for each run and branch size. Olets get a full guide of their own soon.

Reducers (Concentric and Eccentric)

A concentric reducer keeps both ends on the same centreline. An eccentric reducer has one flat side, so the top or bottom of the pipe stays level. At a pump suction, reducers can trap air or vapour, so an eccentric reducer is installed flat side up to eliminate air pockets. When the supply source is above the pump, it is installed flat side down instead (The Process Piping).

Butt Weld Fittings (ASME B16.9)

ASME B16.9Factory-made wrought buttwelding fittings, NPS 1/2 to 48

B16.9 covers the dimensions, tolerances, testing and marking of elbows, tees, crosses, reducers, caps and lap joint stub ends (Project Materials). Fittings are ordered to match the connecting pipe schedule, so a SCH 80 line uses SCH 80 fittings.

They suit larger and high-pressure lines because the full-penetration butt weld gives a smooth bore and can be radiographed. Materials come from separate ASTM specifications.

Socket Weld and Threaded Fittings (ASME B16.11)

ASME B16.11Forged steel fittings, socket welding and threaded, NPS 1/8 to 4

B16.11 fittings are forged and rated by pressure class rather than by schedule (Project Materials). In hydrocarbon piping, socket weld is the usual choice up to about NPS 1-1/2 to 2, threaded joints are generally limited to utility service, and socket weld joints cannot be radiographed (Project Materials, B16.11 vs B16.9).

~40%

The GCC produces around 40% of the world's desalinated water (Oman Observer, February 2020). Desalination, seawater cooling and offshore platforms all need fittings that resist chloride pitting, which is why duplex and super duplex fittings are common on Gulf projects.

Duplex grades have a PREN of around 34 to 35; super duplex grades are near 42, and are used for seawater injection, offshore process piping, subsea lines and high-pressure reverse osmosis feed piping (Project Materials). Super duplex fittings are often made to order, so lead time matters when you plan procurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the types of pipe fittings?
The main types are elbows, tees, reducers, caps, couplings, unions, crosses, swage nipples, olets and stub ends. By end connection they are butt weld (ASME B16.9), socket weld or threaded (ASME B16.11).
What is the difference between a long radius and a short radius elbow?
A long radius elbow has a centre-to-end dimension of 1.5 × NPS; a short radius elbow has 1 × NPS. LR is the default, with lower pressure drop and erosion. SR is used where space is tight or the spec calls for it.
What is the difference between a concentric and an eccentric reducer?
A concentric reducer keeps both ends on one centreline. An eccentric reducer has one flat side, so the top or bottom of the line stays level. Eccentric reducers are used at pump suctions, flat side up, to avoid air pockets.
What is the difference between butt weld and socket weld fittings?
Butt weld fittings (ASME B16.9, NPS 1/2 to 48) are welded end to end with a full-penetration weld that can be radiographed, and match the pipe schedule. Socket weld fittings (ASME B16.11, NPS 1/8 to 4) take the pipe into a socket with a fillet weld, are rated by class, and are used for small bore.
What is an olet in piping?
An olet is an integrally reinforced branch fitting welded directly onto a run pipe to take off a smaller branch, without cutting in a tee. Common types are weldolet, sockolet and threadolet, covered by MSS SP-97.
Which standard covers pipe fittings?
ASME B16.9 for butt weld fittings and ASME B16.11 for forged socket weld and threaded fittings. MSS SP-97 covers olets and MSS SP-95 covers swage nipples. Materials come from ASTM specifications such as A234, A105, A403 and A182.
