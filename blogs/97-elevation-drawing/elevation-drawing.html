An elevation drawing shows one face of a building as if you were standing straight in front of it, with nothing cut away. Below, the same small building is drawn as a plan, a section and an elevation, side by side, so you can see exactly how the three differ.

If you are a student learning to read and draw architectural drawings, these three views are the core of every drawing set. Once you can picture how each one is made, the rest of the set makes sense.

TL;DR

  • A plan is a horizontal cut, usually about 1.2 m above the floor, looking down.
  • A section is a vertical cut through the building, looking sideways in the direction of the arrows on the plan.
  • An elevation cuts nothing: it shows one outside face, with materials, openings and levels.
  • In a section, what is cut is drawn thick or hatched; what you see beyond the cut is drawn thin.
  • A detail drawing zooms in at 1:10 or 1:20 to show how one junction, such as a wall base or window head, is built.
  • In Revit, all of them are views of one model, so they never disagree; in AutoCAD you draw and update each by hand.

Plan, Section and Elevation: The Difference

All three are flat, measured drawings of the same building. The difference is where you cut and which way you look.

What Is an Elevation Drawing?

An elevation is the view of one face of the building, drawn flat, as if you stood directly in front of every point of it at once. There is no perspective, so a window 2 m wide measures 2 m on the drawing at scale, wherever it sits on the façade.

Types of elevation

  • Front, rear and side elevations, one for each face of the building
  • Often named by compass direction (north, south, east, west), the usual practice in UK-style sets
  • Interior elevations of single walls, such as a kitchen or bathroom, usually at a larger scale

What must be labelled

  • Levels: ground, finished floor, top of slab, parapet
  • Heights not shown on the plan, such as sill and lintel
  • Materials and finishes for each surface
  • Opening tags that match the plan and schedules, and gridlines on larger buildings

In the studio elevation above, notice that you can read every height from the level marks on the right: ground at +0.000, finished floor at +0.450, top of slab at +3.600, parapet at +4.500.

What Is a Building Section?

A section is what you would see if you sliced the building vertically and pulled one half away. Its position is shown on the plan by a section line: a chain line with a tag (A-A) at each end, and arrows pointing the way you are looking. First in Architecture notes that sets usually include at least two sections at right angles to each other, and that the section mark on the plan references the drawing number and sheet where the section appears.

CutAnything the cutting plane passes through: walls, slabs, roof, foundations. Drawn with thick lines or hatching.
BeyondThings you see past the cut but do not slice through, such as a window in the far wall. Drawn with thin lines.
HiddenThings behind other surfaces, shown dashed only when they matter.

India's IS 962:1989 asks for two line thicknesses, with thick lines at least twice as thick as thin ones, which is what makes the cut stand out from what lies beyond. A section is also where you read floor-to-floor and floor-to-ceiling heights: in the studio section, the clear height from finished floor to slab soffit is 3,000 mm.

How to Draw Them in AutoCAD and Revit

AutoCAD: draw each view

  1. Draw the plan from the brief or site survey.
  2. Draw construction lines down from the plan to project the elevation, then set heights from the levels.
  3. Draw the section separately from the section line, layer by layer.
  4. Change the design, and redraw the change in every view yourself.

Revit: generate views from one model

  1. Model walls, floors, roof, doors and windows once, in 3D.
  2. Elevations come from elevation markers, and a section from drawing a Section line on the plan.
  3. Cut elements are drawn thick and hatched automatically.
  4. Change the model, and every plan, section and elevation updates.

AutoCAD is still the fastest way to learn projection, because you have to work out every line yourself. Revit is how most practices now produce coordinated sets, because a model cannot have a plan that disagrees with its section.

Reading Drawings on Gulf Projects

Many Gulf consultancies work with British-style drawing practices, and the authorities add their own submission rules. Most of what you learned in India carries over: millimetres for dimensions, the same three views and the same symbols. A few things are worth knowing before your first Gulf set:

Levels
UK practice gives levels in metres to three decimal places, measured from a datum and kept positive (Designing Buildings). Expect labels such as FFL (finished floor level) and FSL (finished structural level).
Datum and coordinates
Site plans tie into the local survey system. Dubai Municipality's survey network uses the DLTM coordinate system. If an absolute datum is used for levels, it must be stated on the drawings.
Elevation names
Elevations are usually titled by compass direction (north, south, east, west) rather than front and rear.
Revisions
Authorities can be strict about showing changes. Dubai Development Authority's submission standards (September 2021) require modifications to be clouded, with approved drawings in yellow clouds and proposals in red.
Drawing numbers
Each authority sets a list of required drawings by discipline, such as architectural A-series and structural S-series sheets, so follow its numbering exactly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an elevation drawing?
An elevation drawing shows one face of a building as if you were standing straight in front of it, with nothing cut away. It shows openings, materials and the levels of the ground, floors, roof and parapet.
What is the difference between a section and an elevation?
A section cuts through the building and shows the inside: walls, floors and roof in cut, with heights. An elevation cuts nothing and shows only the outside face of the building.
What is a building section?
A building section is a vertical cut through a building, drawn as if one half were removed. Its position is marked on the plan by a section line with arrows, and it shows how floors, walls, roof and foundations fit together, with floor-to-floor heights.
What is a detail drawing in architecture?
A detail drawing is a large-scale drawing, usually 1:10 or 1:20, of one junction or element, such as a wall base, stair or window head. It shows every layer, size and material needed to build it.
What scale are elevation drawings drawn at?
Usually the same scale as the floor plans, which IS 962 recommends at 1:50 or 1:100. Interior elevations are often drawn larger to show more detail.
