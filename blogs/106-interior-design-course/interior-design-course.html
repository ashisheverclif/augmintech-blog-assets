Interior design courses in India come at four levels: short certificates (a few weeks to a year), diplomas (one to three years), bachelor's degrees such as a B.Des or B.Sc (three to five years) and master's degrees such as an M.Des (two to two-and-a-half years). Fees range from about ₹34,000 for a full three-year government polytechnic diploma to more than ₹20 lakh at premium private design schools. This guide explains each level, what you will study, real 2026 fees, how admission works and what jobs each course leads to.

TL;DR

  • For a full design career, a bachelor's degree is the standard route: a four-year B.Des or a three- or four-year B.Sc or BID.
  • Diplomas and certificates are faster and cheaper and suit drafting, decor, styling and site roles, or people already working.
  • Fees vary enormously: from ₹33,918 for a full polytechnic diploma to about ₹15.8 lakh for NID's B.Des and over ₹20 lakh at some private schools.
  • Most courses teach the same core: design studio, drawing, materials and construction, services, history, software and professional practice.
  • India has no statutory licence for interior designers, so your portfolio, skills and the course's reputation matter more than the title.
  • Software skills decide early job offers: AutoCAD, SketchUp, a renderer and increasingly Revit.

Bachelor's Degrees: B.Des, B.Sc and BID

A bachelor's degree is the most complete preparation for a design career. You will see three names, and the difference is mostly in emphasis, not in the job you can do afterwards:

B.Des (Interior or Interior Architecture)Design-school model with heavy studio work, design thinking and furniture or product design. NID's B.Des in Furniture and Interior Design is four years; Pearl Academy's B.Des in Interior Architectural Design is listed as three years.Usually needs a design aptitude test
B.Sc Interior DesignUniversity model, often three years, with a balance of studio, technical drawing, construction and management subjects. The University of Mumbai's B.Sc (Hons) Interior Design is a six-semester programme.Usually merit or college test
BID (Bachelor of Interior Design)Professional-style degree, three to five years. Pillai University's BID is four years. CEPT's five-year BID, once well known, has not taken admissions since 2023 according to CEPT's own page.Check admissions are open before applying

Entrance exams. Design schools test drawing, observation, creativity and visual thinking. The best-known is NID's Design Aptitude Test (DAT), with Prelims and Mains stages; for the 2027 intake, applications run from 9 September to 29 November 2026, and the Prelims exam is on 19 December 2026, according to Careers360. Many private institutes run their own tests and portfolio interviews. Start preparing six months ahead with daily sketching, perspective drawing and visual-composition practice.

Diplomas and Certificates

Diplomas and certificates are the fastest and often the cheapest way in. They suit three groups: students after Class 10 who want a practical route, graduates from other fields switching to design, and working people adding a skill.

  • Polytechnic diplomas run for three years after Class 10. Government polytechnics are very affordable; Government Polytechnic Ghaziabad's three-year Interior Decoration diploma is listed at about ₹33,918 in total, with admission through Uttar Pradesh's JEECUP entrance and counselling. Lateral entry is possible after Class 12 or ITI.
  • Private diplomas of one to two years focus on drafting, decor and software, and are offered by many design institutes. Quality varies widely, so check the faculty, studio work and recognition before paying.
  • Certificates last from a few weeks to about a year. Some cover styling and decor, such as Pearl Academy's 11-month Professional Certificate in Styling for Interiors; others teach software such as AutoCAD, SketchUp, Revit or rendering.
Be realistic: a short certificate can get you a drafting or visualisation job, but most design firms expect a degree for designer roles that lead projects. If you start with a diploma, plan how you might add a degree later.

Master's Degrees

A master's suits graduates who want to specialise or teach. NID's M.Des in Furniture and Interior Design takes two and a half years and needs a four-year bachelor's degree; Careers360 lists its total fee at about ₹11.50 lakh. Pearl Academy's two-year M.Des in Interior Design is listed at ₹5,97,800 a year plus a one-time ₹85,000. CEPT also offers a master's in Interior Design. Architects can enter interior master's programmes too, as can designers from related fields.

Do Interior Designers Need a Licence in India?

No. Unlike architects, whose title is protected under the Architects Act 1972, there is no national statutory licensing body for interior designers in India, and the title is not legally protected. The Institute of Indian Interior Designers (IIID), founded in 1972, is a voluntary professional society with a code of conduct; membership is an affiliation, not a legal licence. One important limit: work that affects structure or needs statutory approval must be designed and certified by a licensed architect or structural engineer.

This has a practical consequence for students. Because anyone can call themselves an interior designer, employers judge you on your portfolio, drawings and software skills, and on the reputation of where you studied.

What a Useful First Project Should Teach

Ask a course provider to walk through one compact residential room from measured brief to issued drawings. The learning should connect decisions: why the furniture fits, how the ceiling works, what each finish code means and how the cost follows the specification.

  1. Understand the brief: record users, activities, existing services and budget assumptions before selecting a style.
  2. Test the layout: compare two options using real furniture sizes, door swings and circulation. Explain why one option is stronger.
  3. Document the solution: produce a furniture plan, ceiling plan, internal elevation, finish schedule and one joinery detail.
  4. Revise after feedback: record what changed and recheck every affected drawing. A polished render alone does not prove coordination.

For software training, ask to see the editable files as well as final images. A learner should be able to explain layers and plotting in AutoCAD, groups and components in SketchUp, or rooms, views and schedules in Revit. A practical assessment should test those decisions, not just whether the learner can repeat commands.

Before Paying an Admission Deposit

Obtain the exact qualification name, awarding institution, entry requirements, full fee schedule, refund terms and weekly timetable in writing. Ask which licences and computer specifications are required. Compare feedback hours and assessed project work alongside the programme duration. Confirm that the qualification fits your intended next step, particularly if you plan further study.

How to Choose the Right Course: 10 Questions to Ask

Is it recognised? Check the university's UGC status, or AICTE approval for polytechnic diplomas, before you apply.
Are admissions open? Some well-known programmes have paused intake, as CEPT's BID has.
Who teaches? Look for practising designers and architects among the faculty, not only full-time lecturers.
How much studio time? Ask how many hours a week go to design studio and drawing.
Which software, and how deeply? AutoCAD, SketchUp and rendering at minimum; ask whether Revit is taught.
Is there an internship? A structured internship of at least six to eight weeks is common in good programmes.
Can you see student work? Ask for recent final-year portfolios; they show what the course really produces.
Where do graduates work? Ask for a list of firms that hired last year's batch.
What is the full cost? Tuition plus one-time fees, hostel, materials, software and annual increases.
Does it fit your next step? If you may want a master's later, check that the degree meets those entry rules (NID's M.Des, for example, asks for a four-year bachelor's).

Studying Interior Design With a Gulf Career in Mind

Many Indian interior designers move to Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha or Riyadh, where hotel, retail and office fit-outs are a large part of the work. Employers there typically look for strong working drawings, fit-out detailing and software skills. Larger projects are increasingly BIM-led: since 1 January 2024, Dubai has required BIM model submissions in IFC format for building permits on larger buildings, as summarised on Autodesk's Dubai BIM mandate page. If the Gulf is your goal, choose a course with solid technical drawing, and add Revit before you apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which interior design course is best after Class 12?
For a full design career, a bachelor's degree: a four-year B.Des or a three- or four-year B.Sc or BID. Design schools such as NID admit through an aptitude test; many universities admit on merit or their own test.
How long is an interior design course?
Certificates take a few weeks to about a year, diplomas one to three years, bachelor's degrees three to five years, and master's degrees two to two-and-a-half years.
What are interior design course fees in India?
From about ₹34,000 for a full government polytechnic diploma, to around ₹6–9 lakh for many private university degrees, ₹15.8 lakh for NID's four-year B.Des and over ₹20 lakh at some private design schools, based on published 2026 figures.
Can I do interior design without maths or science?
Often yes: many B.Des and B.Sc programmes accept Class 12 from any stream. Some programmes set subject requirements; CEPT's former BID, for example, asked for Physics and Mathematics. Always check each institute's rules.
Is a licence needed to work as an interior designer in India?
No. There is no statutory licence and the title is not protected. IIID membership is voluntary. Structural or statutory work must be certified by a licensed architect or structural engineer.
Is an online interior design course worth it?
For software skills, often yes. For a full design education, online courses rarely replace studio work, model making, site visits and internships. Use them to add skills, not as your only qualification.
Can an interior designer learn Revit?
Yes, and it is increasingly useful for larger commercial and Gulf projects, where coordinated drawing sets and schedules matter. Students can use Revit free for learning through Autodesk's education plan.
