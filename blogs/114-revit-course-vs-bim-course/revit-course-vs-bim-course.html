A Revit course teaches you a software tool: how to model a building and produce drawings and schedules in Revit. A BIM course teaches a way of working: the standards, information management, coordination and teamwork that BIM projects run on, usually using Revit plus tools such as Navisworks. Revit skills support modelling roles; BIM process skills and project experience support progression towards coordination and management. Many courses use the two names loosely, so the only reliable test is the syllabus. This guide shows what each should cover, who needs which, and how to judge any course before paying.

TL;DR

  • Revit is a tool; BIM is a process. You can know Revit well and still not know how a BIM project is run.
  • A Revit course covers modelling, families, views, sheets and schedules: what a BIM modeller does daily.
  • A BIM course adds ISO 19650 concepts, BIM execution plans, common data environments, LOD, clash detection, 4D/5D and IFC.
  • Start with Revit if you are new; move to BIM coordination once you can model and document a full project.
  • Judge courses by syllabus, project work and the instructor's real BIM experience, not by the title.

The Real Difference

Building information modelling (BIM) is a method of designing, building and operating assets using shared, structured digital information. Revit is one of the main software tools used to create the models. The relationship is like that between accounting and a spreadsheet program: knowing the program is necessary, but it does not make you an accountant.

A Revit course answers

  • How do I model walls, floors, roofs and stairs?
  • How do I create families and parameters?
  • How do I make views, sheets and schedules?
  • How do I export DWG and IFC?

A BIM course answers

  • How is information planned, named, shared and approved?
  • What should a model contain at each stage (LOD)?
  • How do we combine models and resolve clashes?
  • Who is responsible for what, and how is it documented?

What a Good Revit Course Covers

  1. Interface and project setupTemplates, project units, levels and grids, and the project browser.
  2. Building elementsWalls and wall types, floors, roofs, ceilings, doors, windows, stairs, railings and curtain walls.
  3. Rooms and areasRoom placement, area calculation (to wall finish by default), tags and colour schemes.
  4. Views and graphicsPlans, sections, elevations, 3D views, visibility settings and view templates.
  5. FamiliesLoadable families, types and parameters, and editing or creating simple ones.
  6. DocumentationDimensions, annotation, detail components, schedules, sheets and title blocks.
  7. Collaboration basicsLinking DWG and Revit models, worksharing, exporting DWG and IFC.
  8. A full projectA complete building modelled and documented as a drawing set.

Two practical notes. First, ask which Revit version you will learn and whether the institute provides licences; students can use Revit free for learning through Autodesk's education plan. Second, Revit LT lacks several features that BIM work relies on, including worksharing, view filters and Dynamo, according to Autodesk's own comparison, so make sure training uses full Revit.

What a BIM Course Adds

Standards and information management (ISO 19650)

ISO 19650 is the international series of standards for managing information on projects using BIM. Its parts cover concepts and principles, the delivery phase, the operational phase, information exchange and security-minded information management. A BIM course should explain its key ideas:

Information requirementsWhat the client needs: organisational (OIR), project (PIR), asset (AIR) and exchange (EIR) information requirements.
BIM execution plan (BEP)How the team will produce, exchange and manage information on this project.
Common data environment (CDE)The agreed single source of project information, with controlled access and approval states.
Delivery plansThe master and task information delivery plans (MIDP and TIDPs) that schedule who delivers what, when.

Federation, clash detection and 4D

Coordination is where BIM saves real money. Architectural, structural and MEP models are combined into one federated model and checked for conflicts. Autodesk describes Navisworks Manage's Clash Detective as identifying cross-discipline interferences earlier in the design process, and notes it can also test models against laser-scanned point clouds. Linked with TimeLiner, it can run time-based clash checks, which is the basis of 4D construction sequencing. A BIM course should have you run a clash test, review and assign clashes, and track them to resolution.

openBIM and IFC

IFC is the open format for exchanging BIM models between software and with authorities. Dubai, for example, has required BIM model submissions in IFC format for building permits on larger buildings since 1 January 2024, as summarised on Autodesk's Dubai BIM mandate page. A BIM course should cover exporting, checking and reviewing IFC models, not just clicking export.

Can You Learn It Yourself?

Revit basics, yes. Students can use Revit free through Autodesk's education plan, and Autodesk offers free skill-builder and certification-prep courses in its learning pathways. Many professionals learn modelling by rebuilding a project they already know. The BIM process is harder to self-teach, because it is about teamwork: you need other people's models to coordinate with, a BEP to follow, and someone to review your work. That is where structured training with real coordination exercises earns its fee.

One Project, Two Different Assessments

Use the same small office fit-out to compare course outcomes. In a Revit assessment, model the agreed shell and interiors, create rooms, organise views and produce a checked drawing set with schedules. The reviewer should be able to trace the drawings back to model elements and parameters.

In the BIM assessment, add the exchange requirements and team responsibilities. Agree model coordinates and version, receive consultant information, record which files were checked, run an appropriate coordination test and manage one issue through review and rechecking. The output is evidence of a controlled workflow, not only a coloured clash screenshot.

What an Issue Record Should Contain

Record an issue ID, location, relevant model versions, affected elements, a clear description, the responsible party, the proposed action and review status. If a duct intersects a beam, do not move the beam or reduce the duct size simply to remove the clash. The responsible designers must review the engineering implications. Recheck the accepted revision and retain the decision.

Ask for Assessment Evidence

Before enrolling, ask whether the trainer reviews editable files, whether learners receive revision feedback, and whether collaboration is practised with other models. Clarify the required software edition: Autodesk’s Clash Detective documentation identifies it as a Navisworks Manage feature.

ISO 19650-1 concerns information-management concepts and principles. A file-naming exercise alone does not demonstrate the complete process. Similarly, a higher LOD label is not automatically better: the required information should suit the element, purpose and agreed stage.

How to Judge Any Revit or BIM Course

A written, detailed syllabus with topics and hours, not just "Revit + BIM".
A complete project you will model and document, ideally with structure and MEP coordination.
Standards actually taught: ISO 19650 concepts, BEP, naming, LOD, if it calls itself a BIM course.
Coordination tools: clash detection and IFC checking, not only Revit.
Instructors with real project BIM experience, not only software trainers.
Feedback on your work: reviews of your model and drawings, not just videos.
Portfolio output: what you will be able to show an employer at the end.
Licences and version: full Revit (not LT), and which release.
Honest claims: no guaranteed jobs or salaries; clear about any certification and who issues it.

On certification, Autodesk itself runs certification learning pathways and offers free skill-builder and certification-prep courses, so be clear whether a course leads to an Autodesk exam or to the provider's own certificate.

Red Flags in Course Marketing

  1. "BIM course" that only teaches Revit buttons with no standards or coordination.
  2. Guaranteed placement or salary promises. No course can guarantee either.
  3. Vague software lists (ten programs in a few weeks) with no project.
  4. Certificates presented as official without saying who issues them.
  5. No access to sample student work or to the instructors before enrolling.
  6. Training on Revit LT, which lacks worksharing and other BIM features.

A Sensible Learning Path

  1. Revit foundationsModel and document one complete building on your own.
  2. Revit for your disciplineArchitecture, interiors, structure or MEP depth, with families and schedules.
  3. Team BIMWorksharing, linking consultant models, a BIM execution plan and naming standards.
  4. CoordinationFederation, clash detection, IFC exchange and issue tracking.
  5. ManagementStandards, templates, content libraries, CDE workflows and client requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Revit course the same as a BIM course?
No. A Revit course teaches the software; a BIM course teaches the process around it: standards, information management, coordination and teamwork. Some courses use the names loosely, so check the syllabus.
Should I learn Revit or BIM first?
Revit first if you are new. BIM process skills make sense once you can model and document a complete project.
What software does a BIM course use?
Usually Revit for modelling, plus coordination tools such as Navisworks for federation and clash detection, and IFC viewers for openBIM exchange. Some also introduce 4D and 5D tools.
What is ISO 19650?
An international series of standards for managing information on projects using BIM, covering concepts, the delivery and operational phases, information exchange and security.
What is LOD in BIM?
Level of development: how complete and reliable a model element is, from LOD 100 (conceptual) through 200, 300, 350 and 400 to 500 (as-built).
Can I become a BIM coordinator after a course?
A course gives you the knowledge, but coordinator roles usually also need a few years of project experience. Start as a modeller and build towards coordination.
Is Autodesk certification necessary?
Not required, but it can help benchmark your skills. Autodesk offers certification pathways with free prep courses; check whether any course you take leads to an Autodesk exam or its own certificate.
