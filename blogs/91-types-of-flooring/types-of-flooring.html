The six common types of flooring are solid wood, engineered wood, laminate, vinyl, tiles and marble. They differ most in cost, how they handle water and how much upkeep they need. This guide gives you one neutral comparison table with dated Indian prices, then a section on each type and a room-by-room table, so you can pick floors for each room of a home or project without wading through product pitches.

TL;DR

  • Vitrified tiles are India's all-rounder: water-resistant, hard-wearing and about ₹100 to ₹170 per sq ft installed.
  • Wood, solid or engineered, is the warmest and the dearest, at ₹380 to ₹820+ per sq ft installed, and belongs in dry rooms.
  • Laminate looks like wood at about half the price but its fibreboard core swells if water stands on it.
  • SPC vinyl is waterproof and clicks together, which makes it the easy wood-look option for kitchens and offices.
  • Marble needs polishing every 3 to 4 years; granite is tougher but gets slippery when wet and polished.
  • In Gulf homes, porcelain tiles, marble and SPC handle heat, sand and constant AC better than solid wood.

Wooden Flooring

Interior and product images are AI-generated illustrations. Click any image to enlarge.

Wooden flooring comes in two forms, and the difference matters more than the species.

Solid wood is a plank cut from a single piece of timber. Because it is wood all the way through, it can be sanded and refinished many times over its life. Engineered wood has a thin top layer of real hardwood, the veneer or wear layer, glued onto a cross-laminated plywood core. From above, the two look identical. The layered core moves less than a solid plank as humidity changes, which is why engineered boards are preferred over underfloor heating.

How often you can resand an engineered floor depends on the veneer. The Wooden Floor Store estimates that each sanding removes about 1 mm:

Veneer under 2 mm0 sandings
2–3 mm1, with care
3–4 mmAbout 2
4 mm and over2 to 4

Specs are written as total/veneer thickness: 15/4 means a 4 mm veneer on a 15 mm board.

Pros
  • Warm, quiet and real wood underfoot
  • Can be refinished (solid: many times)
  • Adds resale appeal
Cons
  • The priciest floor here, alongside imported marble
  • Swells, cups or stains with water
  • Scratches from grit and pet claws

Why bedrooms, not bathrooms: bedrooms are dry, often air-conditioned and see light foot traffic, which is where wood lasts. Bathrooms and kitchens bring standing water and steam. JK Cement notes that engineered wood swells with moisture unless it is laid over a vapour barrier, and recommends it only for dry, air-conditioned rooms.

Vinyl Flooring

Vinyl flooring is made from PVC with a printed design and a clear wear layer on top. It comes in three formats.

Rolls

Sheet vinyl

Wide rolls glued to the subfloor with few seams. Cheapest, and common in clinics, labs and budget rooms.

Flexible planks

LVT / LVP

Luxury vinyl tiles and planks, thin and flexible, usually glued down. Wood and stone looks.

Rigid click

SPC

Stone-plastic composite core makes a rigid plank that clicks together over a flat floor. Marketed as waterproof.

The wear layer is the clear top film that takes the scuffs, and its thickness tells you where a vinyl floor can go. Alpine Floor's guide (June 2026) breaks it down like this:

0.3 mm
Bedrooms, living rooms, apartments and rentals; light retail
0.5 mm
Kitchens, hallways, small offices; the usual commercial standard
0.7 mm
Heavy commercial: retail floors, public corridors, hospitals, schools

Vinyl's weak points are that it cannot be sanded or refinished, cheaper products can dent under heavy furniture, and quality varies widely between brands, so check the datasheet for the wear layer and core type before buying.

Laminate Flooring

Laminate flooring is a photograph of wood or stone sealed under a hard, clear overlay and bonded to a high-density fibreboard (HDF) core, with a balancing layer underneath (see the diagram above). The planks click together and float over an underlay.

Laminate is graded by an AC (abrasion class) rating from the Association of European Producers of Laminate Flooring. The higher the number, the more traffic it takes:

Tiles and Marble

Tiles and stone are still India's most common floors, because they shrug off water, heat and heavy cleaning.

Vitrified tiles

Fired until glass-like, absorbing 0.5% water or less. Hard, stain-resistant and suitable for every room; choose matt or anti-skid in wet areas.

₹100–170 installed (HouseYog)

Ceramic tiles

Cheaper and more porous than vitrified, so better for walls, bathrooms and light-traffic floors than for busy halls.

₹65–110 installed (HouseYog)

Marble

Natural stone with unique veining. Indian marble such as Makrana costs far less than imported Italian or Turkish stone. It is porous, so seal it, and wipe spills such as lemon juice quickly.

₹120–230 Indian, ₹280–600+ imported, installed (HouseYog)

Granite

Harder than marble and rated very high for durability. Used on stairs, entrances and balconies; a highly polished finish gets slippery when wet.

₹130–350 installed (HouseYog)

Flooring for Gulf Homes

UAE and Saudi homes put floors through conditions Indian homes rarely face at the same time: outdoor summer temperatures that regularly exceed 45°C, fine sand tracked indoors, and air conditioning that runs most of the year. Dubai flooring contractor Floorworld (July 2026) points to the constant swing between outdoor heat and air-conditioned interiors as the reason to match the material to the climate.

Heat and sunPorcelain and vitrified tiles have low thermal movement and do not fade under UV, which is why tile suppliers recommend them for terraces and sunlit rooms.
SandFine sand acts like sandpaper on soft surfaces. Pick a thick vinyl wear layer or an AC4 laminate, and put mats at every entrance.
AC humidity swingsWood moves as indoor humidity changes. Solid wood is the riskiest; engineered wood is safer if it is acclimatised and the AC keeps rooms steady.

In practice that points to porcelain tiles or marble for living areas and wet rooms, with SPC vinyl as the wood-look choice. Solid wood is best kept to well-sealed, air-conditioned bedrooms, if used at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which type of flooring is best for home in India?
Vitrified tiles are the best all-round choice for most Indian homes: they resist water and stains, take heavy cleaning and cost about ₹100 to ₹170 per sq ft installed. Add engineered wood or laminate in bedrooms if you want warmth underfoot.
What is the difference between vinyl and laminate flooring?
Laminate has a fibreboard (HDF) core and is rated AC1 to AC5 for wear; it swells if water stands on it. Vinyl is made from PVC, rated by wear layer thickness, and SPC vinyl has a waterproof rigid core, so it suits kitchens and other wet-prone rooms.
Is wooden flooring good for Indian weather?
It works in dry, air-conditioned rooms such as bedrooms. Humid monsoon months make wood swell and shrink, so engineered wood is the safer choice, laid over a vapour barrier, and kept out of kitchens, bathrooms and ground floors prone to damp.
Which flooring is waterproof?
Vitrified tiles, which absorb 0.5% water or less, and SPC vinyl are the most water-resistant floors. Wood and laminate are not waterproof, and marble needs sealing to resist stains.
What is engineered wood flooring?
Engineered wood is a plank with a thin top layer of real hardwood, the veneer, glued to a plywood core. It looks like solid wood but moves less with humidity, and can be sanded up to a few times depending on veneer thickness.
