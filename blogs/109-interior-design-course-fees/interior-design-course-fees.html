Interior design course fees in India range from about ₹34,000 for an entire three-year government polytechnic diploma to more than ₹20 lakh for a private design-school degree. Private university degrees typically cost ₹6–9 lakh in total, NID's four-year B.Des about ₹15.8 lakh, master's degrees ₹11–13 lakh, and professional certificates around ₹4.5 lakh. Software training costs far less, and students can use key software free or cheaply. This guide compares real 2026 fees, shows the hidden costs, and helps you judge value before you pay.

TL;DR

  • Government polytechnic diplomas are by far the cheapest: about ₹33,918 for three years at Government Polytechnic Ghaziabad.
  • Private university degrees cost roughly ₹1.5–2.5 lakh a year: about ₹6 lakh at Pillai University and ₹8.9 lakh at DIT University for four years.
  • Design schools cost the most: about ₹15.8 lakh for NID's B.Des and ₹7.3 lakh a year at Pearl Academy, plus one-time fees.
  • Master's degrees cost ₹11–13 lakh: ₹11.5 lakh at NID and about ₹12.8 lakh at Pearl Academy.
  • Budget 10–20% more than tuition for a laptop, software after student licences, materials, printing, travel and hostel.
  • Higher fees do not guarantee better outcomes: compare faculty, studio time, internships and graduate portfolios.

The 10–20% figure is an Augmintech planning rule of thumb, not survey data; see the hidden-costs section for what it covers.

Degree Fees: Why the Range Is So Wide

Three kinds of institutes offer interior design degrees, and they price very differently:

Private universitiesTypically about ₹1.5–2.5 lakh a year. Pillai University quotes about ₹1.5 lakh a year for its four-year BID; DIT University's B.Des Interior Design runs from ₹2.07 lakh to ₹2.43 lakh a year.~₹6–9 lakh for 4 years
National design institutesNID's B.Des in Furniture and Interior Design is about ₹15.8 lakh over four years, with only 13 seats and entry through the competitive DAT.~₹15.8 lakh for 4 years
Premium private design schoolsPearl Academy lists ₹7.32 lakh a year for its B.Des in Interior Architectural Design, plus ₹85,000 one-time, and says fees rise annually.~₹30.14 lakh for 3 years

What drives the difference: studio size and faculty ratio, workshops and materials labs, campus facilities, industry projects and the brand's network. Some of that genuinely improves learning; some is the cost of a premium campus. Ask what your fee buys, in hours of studio teaching and access to workshops, not just facilities on a brochure.

Diploma and Certificate Fees

Government polytechnic diplomas are the most affordable formal route. Government Polytechnic Ghaziabad's three-year Interior Decoration diploma is listed at about ₹33,918 in total, with admission through Uttar Pradesh's JEECUP entrance exam. Other state polytechnics are similarly low-cost; check your state's polytechnic admission portal.

Private diplomas of one to two years are offered by many design institutes and vary widely in price and quality. Because there is no single regulator for short interior courses, compare them on faculty, studio work, software taught and student portfolios rather than fees alone.

Professional certificates sit in between. Pearl Academy's 11-month Professional Certificate in Styling for Interiors costs ₹4,44,778 including GST, and Pearl says it offers a six-month no-cost EMI plan for its professional certificates through GrayQuest.

Value check: a ₹4–5 lakh certificate costs as much as half a private university degree. If you want a long-term design career, compare it against a recognised diploma or degree before committing.

Is a More Expensive Course Better?

Not automatically. Fees reflect brand, location and facilities as much as teaching quality. Judge value on what you actually get:

Studio hours per week and the number of students per studio teacher.
Who teaches: practising designers and architects, not only full-time lecturers.
Internships: structured, credited and long enough to work on real drawings.
Software depth: AutoCAD, SketchUp, rendering and ideally Revit, taught through projects.
Graduate portfolios: ask to see recent final-year work, including working drawings.
Recognition: UGC-recognised university, or AICTE-approved polytechnic for diplomas.
Total cost: tuition, one-time fees, annual increases, hostel and extras added up.

Fees vs Starting Salary: An Honest Look

Interior design is a rewarding field, but starting pay is modest. PayScale reports about ₹2.5 lakh a year for interior designers with under a year's experience and about ₹3.3 lakh on average across all levels (see our interior designer salary guide). Set against that:

Augmintech arithmetic: full-course tuition divided by ₹2.5 lakh. It ignores living costs, pay growth and the network a school provides, so treat it as a rough reality check, not a verdict.

The point is not that expensive courses are bad. It is that the fee should match your plans: a premium school makes most sense if you will use its network for competitive roles, while a lower-cost degree plus strong software skills and a sharp portfolio can reach similar first jobs.

Fees for Working Professionals

If you already work, a full-time degree may not be practical. Part-time diplomas, weekend certificates and online software courses cost less and let you keep earning. Check three things before paying: whether the credential is recognised for what you want to do next, how many hours of live teaching and feedback you get, and whether you finish with real drawings for your portfolio. For a degree you will rely on, confirm the university's recognition directly with the regulator's list, not the brochure.

Ways to Pay for an Interior Design Course

  • Institute scholarships. Many institutes offer merit or need-based scholarships; Pearl Academy and Pillai University both mention them. Ask for the criteria in writing.
  • Education loans. Most banks offer education loans for recognised courses. Compare interest, collateral and repayment start dates; terms change often.
  • EMI plans. Some institutes offer instalments for certificates, such as Pearl's six-month no-cost EMI.
  • Earn while you learn. Freelance drafting or 3D work in later years can offset costs, using properly licensed software.
  • Start lower, add later. A polytechnic diploma or affordable degree followed by targeted software training can cost a fraction of a premium degree.

Worked Example: Compare the Same Total Cost

The following is a fictional budgeting exercise, not a provider quotation. Suppose a one-year course charges ₹1,20,000 tuition, ₹8,000 compulsory academic charges and ₹12,000 for materials and printing. Local travel adds ₹18,000 and an incremental laptop purchase adds ₹60,000. The study budget is ₹2,18,000 before any applicable taxes, finance charges, accommodation or lost earnings.

If you already own a suitable laptop, the incremental budget becomes ₹1,58,000. This is why a fixed percentage above tuition can be misleading. Use the actual costs of your situation and avoid counting an existing purchase as a new cash expense.

Questions Behind a “Software Included” Offer

Ask whether the fee covers instruction, a temporary lab login, an individual licence or only help applying for education access. Confirm the application edition, licence duration, renewal conditions and whether commercial use is permitted. A learning licence should not be assumed to cover freelance work. Check render plugins separately from the modelling application.

Get the Payment Terms in Writing

Request the full-course fee, instalment dates, refundable deposit, cancellation policy and all compulsory charges. For financed payments, compare total repayment, processing charges and interest, not just the monthly amount. Do not treat advertised placement assistance as a guaranteed salary or a guaranteed way to repay borrowing. Keep the signed quotation and terms with your receipt.

8 Questions to Ask Before You Pay

  1. What is the total fee for the whole course, including one-time charges?
  2. How much do fees rise each year? Pearl Academy, for example, states that fees increase annually.
  3. What is the refund policy if you withdraw in the first weeks?
  4. Are study tours, materials and software included or charged separately?
  5. Which campus and academic year does the published fee apply to?
  6. Is GST added to any part of the fee?
  7. What scholarships exist, and when must you apply?
  8. Can you see last year's student portfolios and a list of firms that hired graduates?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fee for an interior design course in India?
From about ₹34,000 for an entire government polytechnic diploma, to about ₹6–9 lakh for many private university degrees, ₹15.8 lakh for NID's B.Des and over ₹20 lakh at premium private design schools, based on published 2026 figures.
What is NID's fee for interior design?
Careers360 lists NID's four-year B.Des in Furniture and Interior Design at about ₹15.83 lakh in total and its two-and-a-half-year M.Des at about ₹11.50 lakh. Confirm current fees on NID's admissions notice.
Which is the cheapest interior design course?
A government polytechnic diploma. Government Polytechnic Ghaziabad's three-year Interior Decoration diploma is listed at about ₹33,918 in total.
Is a diploma enough to become an interior designer?
It can get you into drafting, decor and site roles, especially with strong software skills and a good portfolio. Many design firms prefer a degree for designer roles that lead projects.
How much does software training cost?
The software is free or cheap for students: Autodesk tools are free through the education plan and SketchUp costs US$55 a year. Training ranges from free tutorials to paid structured programmes; pay for courses that include real projects and feedback.
Are interior design fees worth it?
They can be, if the course gives you strong studio teaching, technical drawing, software skills and internships. Starting salaries are modest, so match the fee to your plans and compare graduate portfolios, not brochures.
